## Magic Bus — the distress and the rescue (U.S. Coast Guard + news)
- USCG press release "Coast Guard rescues 5 people from sinking sailboat off Cape Hatteras": https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4328992/coast-guard-rescues-5-people-from-sinking-sailboat-off-cape-hatteras/
  - Thursday Nov. 6, 2025; sailing vessel Magic Bus taking on water ~260 nautical miles offshore Cape Hatteras, N.C.
  - Sector North Carolina Command Center received the distress call; crew reported they were abandoning ship into the life raft
  - Cutter Angela McShan (WPC 1135) diverted; HC-130 Hercules and MH-60 Jayhawk launched from Air Station Elizabeth City
  - HC-130 located the life raft, established communications, confirmed all five stable and in good condition
  - Coordinated with aircraft carrier USS George H.W. Bush for refueling support; MH-60 recovered all five in good health and flew them to Air Station Elizabeth City
  - No weather figures, no times of day, no position, no quotes in the release

## Magic Bus — the passage (crew's incident report, as reviewed by S/V Sabado)
- https://svsabado.com/offshore-nightmare-decisions-that-saved-the-crew/ (review by Holly Hendricks of the S/V Magic Bus incident report, Feb. 22, 2026). Script attributes these points to "a review of the crew's own incident report":
  - departed Stonington Nov. 3, 2025, "promising weather window" / good weather forecasts
  - Nov. 4: water rising in the port engine compartment; jib sheet failed, damaging a hatch, leaving the boat under bare poles
  - flooding outpaced buckets, electric pumps and a converted raw-water intake engine pump
  - both engines inoperable, eliminating steerage; generator overheated and batteries drained as the pumps ran
  - early Nov. 6: water 18 inches over the port hull cabin sole; crew abandoned ship to the raft
  - MH-60 deployed a rescue swimmer; vessel later found inverted, port rudder skeg missing; likely struck a submerged object while sailing at more than 10 knots in rough seas
  - Not used: claims about which onboard devices did or did not transmit (secondary summary; not confirmed by the Coast Guard)

## Background
- Graveyard of the Atlantic (National Park Service, Cape Hatteras National Seashore): https://www.nps.gov/caha/learn/historyculture/shipwrecks.htm — Diamond Shoals: shifting shallow sandbars extending eight miles out; cold Labrador Current and warm Gulf Stream converge just offshore; hurricanes and nor'easters; vessels lost near Cape Hatteras "estimated at over 1,000"
- MH-60T fact sheet (U.S. Coast Guard Acquisitions, CG-9): https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/Factsheets/MH-60T.pdf — range 700 nm, endurance 6.5 hours, 170 knots
- Aviation Survival Technician: https://gocoastguard.com/careers/enlisted/ast — 22-week course: 7-week EMT, 10-week rescue swimmer, 5-week aviation life support technician
- NOAA SARSAT system overview: https://www.sarsat.noaa.gov/sys-diag.html — 406 MHz signal with unique HEX ID; LEO/MEO/GEO satellites; local user terminal computes the location; Mission Control Center routes to the Rescue Coordination Center; "almost instantaneously"; "much quicker when the beacon is properly registered"
- Our calculations: 260 nm ≈ 299 statute miles; Air Station Elizabeth City (36.26N, 76.18W) to Cape Hatteras (35.25N, 75.53W) ≈ 79 statute miles / 68 nm great-circle (script: "about seventy miles"). Map position of Magic Bus is illustrative (the Coast Guard released the distance only); marker placed 260 nm from the cape on an assumed bearing and labelled as illustrative.

## December 30, 2025 — two people, a dog and a cat
- DVIDS 992015 / 992017 description: two people, their dog and their cat rescued Dec. 30, 2025 by an Air Station Elizabeth City helicopter crew near Cape Hatteras; sailboat disabled; activated their EPIRB. (DVIDS text says "MH-60 Dolphin" — the MH-60 is the Jayhawk; news reports say MH-60/Jayhawk.) https://www.dvidshub.net/video/992015 · https://www.dvidshub.net/video/992017
- WTKR (Will Thomas): https://www.wtkr.com/news/in-the-community/outer-banks/coast-guard-saves-two-people-cat-and-dog-65-miles-offshore-of-cape-hatteras
  - distress call early Tuesday morning, ~65 miles offshore of Cape Hatteras, 40-foot catamaran; four-person helicopter crew from Air Station Elizabeth City; about one hour to the scene
  - USCG post: "The vessel had a torn jib sail but was able to make progress towards land using their diesel engine"; found a small hole; seas up to 18 feet; activated EPIRB
  - AST2 James Butz (rescue swimmer four years) swam to the boat; first survivor took the cat in a dry bag; basket; Butz swam back for the second survivor and the dog; pilots kept the helicopter steady, flight mechanic lowered and recovered everyone
  - quotes: "It was my first dog and my first cat." · "The dog had flotation as well, so it made our job super easy," · "It was about 30 to 40 mile an hour winds. It was about 10 to 15 foot seas," · "They call it the Graveyard of the Atlantic, for a reason." · "Having a working EPIRB like these people did, having proper flotation is very key to safety," · "I think it's a rite of passage for every rescue swimmer, to be able to get some pets,"

- Coast Guard Mutual Assistance (Donnie Brzuska): https://mycgma.org/coast-guard-rescue-two-mariners-dog-cat-cape-hatteras/ — about 65 miles southeast of Cape Hatteras; MH-60 Jayhawk; watchstanders received the distress signal and position "within minutes"; crew could fly directly; two hoists: first a mariner and the cat (dry bag), second the other mariner and the dog (flotation vest); swimmer in the water, flight mechanic operated the hoist from the cabin door, pilots held the aircraft steady
