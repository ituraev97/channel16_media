## The cooler rescue (Capt. Dave), Oct 7–10, 2024
- U.S. Coast Guard release, Oct 10, 2024, "Coast Guard rescues man off Longboat Key following Hurricane Milton" — https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/3933279/coast-guard-rescues-man-off-longboat-key-following-hurricane-milton/
  - Thursday: Air Station Miami helicopter crew rescued a man ~30 miles off Longboat Key; found ~1:30 p.m. clinging to a cooler and wearing a life jacket; taken to Tampa General Hospital.
  - Air Station Clearwater's airplane and helicopter crews had deployed to ATC Mobile for storm avoidance; Air Station Miami launched ~5:30 a.m. to search for the missing captain.
  - Monday ~12:40 p.m.: captain of F/V Capt. Dave reported he and a crew member were disabled ~20 miles off John's Pass; Station Sand Key boat + Air Station Clearwater helicopter responded; helicopter hoisted both, returned to Air Station Clearwater in good condition; vessel left adrift.
  - Wednesday ~noon: owner reported the captain had gone back out ~3 a.m. to make repairs and had not checked in; watchstanders reached him by radio; rudder fouled with a line, disabled while returning to port.
  - Seas 6–8 ft, winds ~30 mph, deteriorating; told to don a life jacket and stay with the vessel's EPIRB; communication lost ~6:45 p.m. Wednesday.
  - Lt. Cmdr. Dana Grady (Sector St. Petersburg command center chief): est. 75–90 mph winds, 20–25 ft seas for an extended period incl. overnight; survived because of a life jacket, EPIRB and a cooler; "This man survived in a nightmare scenario for even the most experienced mariner."
- NPR / GPB, Oct 11, 2024, "'We were ecstatic': Coast Guard rescues a man clinging to a cooler in open water" — https://www.gpb.org/news/2024/10/11/we-were-ecstatic-coast-guard-rescues-man-clinging-cooler-in-open-water
  - Pilots Lt. Landon Klopfenstein and Lt. Ian Logan (Miami-based); searched with description + search pattern, limited fuel; another helicopter on same pattern; crew told to return to coast; direction-finding equipment picked up beacon signal, bursts about once a minute; followed bearing lines; swimmer saw object ~2 o'clock; arms raised; hoisted ~1:30 p.m. Thursday, ~17 hours after landfall; last contact less than three hours before landfall (NPR) — landfall time per WWSB below gives <2 h.
  - Quotes: "It's a lot of math that determines where we're gonna go and search because we have limited resources, limited fuel" (Logan); "As we were trucking that way, our swimmer noticed some debris or an object about 2 o'clock on the aircraft" (Klopfenstein); "a miracle"; "We couldn't believe it, honestly, that he was OK"; "Survival equipment is priceless."
- FOX Weather — https://www.foxweather.com/extreme-weather/florida-gulf-of-mexico-cooler-coast-guard-rescue-milton (Monday rescue, boat left adrift, salvage arranged; contact lost 6:45 p.m., landfall just under two hours later)
- Military.com (AFP) — https://military.com/daily-news/2024/10/11/us-coast-guard-rescues-man-hanging-cooler-off-florida-coast.html

## EPIRB
- NOAA SARSAT FAQ: an EPIRB "can be automatically or manually activated to transmit a distress signal to a satellite" — https://www.sarsat.noaa.gov/faq%202.html
- NPR (above): the crew's direction-finding equipment picked up an alert/signal in bursts about once a minute; NPR does NOT say it was his EPIRB — the script calls it an unidentified signal.
