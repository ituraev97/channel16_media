## t1 — THAAD (Missile Defense Agency flight tests)
Page: Missile Defense Agency, THAAD — https://www.mda.mil/system/thaad.html
- "Terminal High Altitude Area Defense"; land-based element that intercepts ballistic missiles in their final (terminal) phase of flight, inside or just outside the atmosphere.
- "Hit-to-kill" technology: kinetic energy destroys the incoming warhead.
- Launcher: truck-mounted, mobile; interceptors can be fired and reloaded quickly; eight interceptors per launcher.
- Radar: AN/TPY-2, largest air-transportable X-band radar in the world; searches, tracks, discriminates and feeds tracking data to the interceptor.
- Test record: 17 successful intercepts in 17 attempts.
- Eight batteries procured; seven fielded to the U.S. Army, the eighth in production.
Page: Joint Chiefs of Staff news, "THAAD Successfully Intercepts 15th Target" (July 31, 2017) — https://www.jcs.mil/Media/News/News-Display/Article/1262992/thaad-successfully-intercepts-15th-target/
- Medium-range target ballistic missile (MRBM) air-launched by a U.S. Air Force C-17 over the Pacific Ocean; THAAD at Pacific Spaceport Complex Alaska, Kodiak, detected, tracked and intercepted it (same text in DVIDS 540934, test FET-01, July 30, 2017).
Page: MDA release July 11, 2017 (mirror) — https://www.globalsecurity.org/space/library/news/2017/space-170711-mda01.htm
- July 11, 2017: threat-representative intermediate-range ballistic missile (IRBM) target air-launched by a C-17 north of Hawaii; THAAD at Pacific Spaceport Complex Alaska, Kodiak, intercepted it. (July 30 − July 11 = 19 days.)
Page: MDA release Aug 30, 2019 (mirror) — https://www.globalsecurity.org/space/library/news/2019/space-190830-mda01.htm
- FTT-23: first use of a government-developed remote launcher kit, which extends the defended area; "16th successful intercept in 16 attempts".
- DVIDS 705868 on-screen slates: "THAAD interceptor launch from the Reagan Test Site, Kwajalein Atoll in the Republic of the Marshall Islands", "Flight Test THAAD-23, August 30, 2019".

Footage: 705868 (FTT-23, Kwajalein, Aug 30, 2019), 540934 (FET-01, Kodiak, July 30, 2017: C-17 air launch of the MRBM target, interceptor launch, intercept), 537462 (FTT-18, Kodiak, July 11, 2017: interceptor launch, IRBM target, intercept). Slates and MDA logos are not used.

## t2 — M109A7 Paladin
Page: DOT&E FY2018 annual report, M109A7 Paladin Integrated Management — https://www.dote.osd.mil/Portals/97/pub/reports/FY2018/army/2018m109pim.pdf
- Tracked, self-propelled 155 mm howitzer (M109 Family of Vehicles).
- Mission: destroy, defeat or disrupt the enemy with integrated, massed, precision indirect fires in support of maneuver units.
- M992A3 Carrier Ammunition Tracked resupplies the howitzer; carries 12,000 lb or 98 rounds (its own crew is four; the howitzer crew figure comes from Army Technology below).
- Army plans to field up to 689 sets of the M109 FoV. Contractor BAE Systems, York, Pa.
Page: Army Technology, "Paladin M109A7 155mm artillery system" — https://www.army-technology.com/projects/paladin-m109a7-155mm-artillery-system/
- Crew of four; 155mm M284 cannon.
- Rate of fire: sustained 1 round/min, maximum 4 rounds/min.
- Range: 22 km standard projectiles, 30 km rocket-assisted projectiles.
- Engine 675 hp; road speed 61 km/h; cannon mounted on a chassis structure common to the Bradley Fighting Vehicle.
- Low-rate initial production began May 2014; first vehicle delivered April 2015.

Footage (DVIDS descriptions in dvids/): 944609 (Fort Cavazos, Texas, Nov 20, 2024 — "Paladins firing powerful artillery rounds downrange and Soldiers working as a team to load rounds"), 954992 (Marne Focus, HIMARS and M109A7 Paladin live fire, Fort Stewart, Ga., Mar 11, 2025), 955158 (Pabrade Training Area, Lithuania, Mar 6, 2025 — German soldiers sent targeting information to U.S. Soldiers), 864039 (Toruń, Poland, Nov 3, 2022 live fire).

## t3 — B-1B Lancer
Page: Air Force Global Strike Command fact sheet, B-1B Lancer — https://www.afgsc.af.mil/About/Fact-Sheets/Article/630718/b-1b-lancer/
- Largest conventional payload of both guided and unguided weapons in the Air Force inventory.
- Variable-geometry wings: forward settings for takeoff, landing, air refueling and some high-altitude weapons employment; aft (swept) settings for high subsonic and supersonic flight.
- Engines: four General Electric F101-GE-102 turbofans with afterburner, each over 30,000 lbf.
- Wingspan 137 ft extended forward, 79 ft swept aft; length 146 ft; max takeoff weight 477,000 lb; payload 75,000 lb.
- Speed: 900-plus mph (Mach 1.2 at sea level).
- Crew: four (aircraft commander, copilot, two combat systems officers).
- Unit cost: $317 million. Inventory: active force 44 (2 test).
- First B-1B delivered to Dyess AFB, Texas, June 1985; final one May 1988.
- Nuclear mission eliminated in 1994 (conventional-only conversion completed March 2011).
- Holds almost 50 world records for speed, payload, range and time to climb in its class.

Footage: 873471 (Andersen AFB, Guam, Cope North, Feb 10, 2023), 453808 (Dyess AFB, B-1 takeoffs with afterburners, 2016), 573931 (Dyess AFB, Exercise Global Thunder 18, nighttime afterburners, Nov 2017), 914029 (Luleå-Kallax Air Base, Sweden, Bomber Task Force 24-2, Feb 26, 2024), 849317 (Dyess AFB, June 29, 2022, 37th anniversary of the B-1 arrival at Dyess), 1004381 (53rd Wing, Eglin AFB, 2026; only shots without its burned-in graphics are used).

## t4 — Harpoon / ex-USS Peleliu SINKEX
DVIDS 1017152 / 1015815 description: decommissioned amphibious assault ship USS Peleliu (LHA 5) struck by two UGM-84 Harpoon anti-ship cruise missiles fired from Los Angeles-class fast-attack submarine USS Columbia (SSN 771), during a live-fire sinking exercise, RIMPAC 2026, Pacific Ocean, July 17, 2026; over 35 years of naval service; displacement of nearly 40,000 tons.
Page: UK Defence Journal, "American assault ship hit by missiles sinks in Pacific exercise" (Aug 4, 2026) — https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/american-assault-ship-hit-by-missiles-sinks-in-pacific-exercise/
- Unmanned surface vessels also took part; two UGM-84 Harpoons from USS Columbia.
- Unmanned surface vessels also took part against the hulk. U.S. Navy reports the ship was sunk; rests about 15,000 ft down, more than 50 nautical miles off the northern coast of Kauai.
- U.S. environmental rules require sinkings in water at least 6,000 ft deep and at least 50 nm from land.
Page: U.S. Navy fact file, Harpoon — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2168358/harpoon-missile
- All-weather, over-the-horizon anti-ship missile; active radar guidance; terminal sea-skimming and pop-up modes; launched from surface ships, submarines, shore batteries or aircraft; A/R/UGM-84 first introduced 1977.
Page: NAVAIR, Harpoon — https://www.navair.navy.mil/product/Harpoon
- Surface/submarine-launched length 15 ft; weight 1,523 lb with booster; Teledyne turbojet/solid-propellant booster for surface and submarine launch; 29 foreign military partners employ Harpoon Block II.

## t5 — CV-22 Osprey
Page: U.S. Air Force fact sheet, CV-22 Osprey — https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104531/cv-22-osprey/
- Mission: long-range infiltration, exfiltration and resupply for special operations forces. Special Operations variant of the Marine MV-22.
- Tiltrotor: takes off and lands vertically like a helicopter; once airborne the nacelles tilt forward and it flies with the range, speed and fuel efficiency of a turboprop.
- Two Rolls-Royce Liberty AE1107C engines, each over 6,200 shaft horsepower; rotor diameter 38 ft; wingspan 83 ft 10 in.
- Max speed 280 knots; ceiling 25,000 ft; combat radius 500 nm with one internal auxiliary fuel tank.
- Crew four (pilot, copilot, two flight engineers); 24 seated personnel, 32 floor-loaded, or 10,000 lb cargo.
- First operational aircraft delivered January 2007; IOC 2009. Unit cost $90 million.

Footage: 482116 (15th SOS MC-130H Combat Talon II tilt-rotor air-to-air refueling with 8th SOS CV-22Bs, Hurlburt Field, Fla., Sept 7, 2016), 675255 (CV-22 of the 352d Special Operations Wing, RAF Mildenhall, routine takeoff and landing, 2019), 635383 / 635387 (CV-22 takeoff / landing on runway, U.S. Air Force courtesy video, Oct 2018).
