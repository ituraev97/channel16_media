## Official fact sheets / articles
- [NATO-PATRIOT] NATO fact sheet "PATRIOT" (Dec 2012) — https://www.nato.int/nato_static_fl2014/assets/pdf/pdf_2012_12/20121204_121204-factsheet-patriot-en.pdf
  - surface-to-air guided air and missile defence system; "Phased Array Tracking Radar to Intercept on Target"; U.S. forces deployed the first PATRIOT systems in the mid-1980s
  - battery: radar set, engagement control station (calculates interceptor trajectories, controls launch sequence; the only manned station in a fire unit), launcher stations (each carries 4 PAC-2 or 16 PAC-3 missiles), antenna mast group, power plant
  - PAC-2 detonates near the threat (proximity fuse); PAC-3 destroys targets by direct impact, "Hit-to-Kill"
  - radar range 150+ km; speed 5,000 km/h (≈ 3,107 mph → "more than three thousand miles an hour"); missile length 5.2 m; weight 320 kg
- [MDA-PAC3] Missile Defense Agency, PAC-3 page (archived 2013) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130216123956/http://www.mda.mil/system/pac_3.html
  - PAC-3 "employs hit-to-kill technology"; fielded by the U.S. Army
- [ARMY-POLAND] army.mil, "Over 100 Bradleys from 405th AFSB, TLSC-E issued to Cavalry Soldiers for Poland rotation" (Sept 2025) — https://www.army.mil/article/288321/
  - M2A3 carries infantry to key areas of the battlefield and provides fire support; 25mm M242 Bushmaster chain gun, TOW missile launcher, 7.62mm M240C machine gun; optics and electronics for target acquisition; carries six infantrymen plus a three-man crew
  - over 100 M2A3 Bradleys issued to 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment, for the Poland rotation (issue Sept 5, 2025)
- [ARMY-BULGARIA] army.mil, "1-16th Infantry Completes Bradley Live Fire Qualification in Bulgaria" (2025) — https://www.army.mil/article/288869/
  - M242 Bushmaster 25mm chain gun; six gunnery tables, day and night iterations; completing crew gunnery makes crews eligible for squad, section and platoon live fire qualifications
- [ARMY-KUWAIT] army.mil, "Armor company conducts crew gunnery qualifications" (May 2015) — https://www.army.mil/article/147968/
  - M2A3 crews qualified in both day and night iterations
- [CMH-BRADLEY] U.S. Army Center of Military History, "M2/M3 Series Bradley Fighting Vehicle" (archived army.mil page) — https://webdoc.gwdg.de/ebook/p/2005/CMH_2/www.army.mil/cmh-pg/books/www/256.htm
  - designed to operate "with the same speed as the M1A1 Abrams" (spoken: "built to keep pace with the M1 Abrams tank"); crew 3; 25mm cannon; TOW (two launch tubes); 7.62mm machine gun
- [TYNDALL-F22] Tyndall AFB fact sheet, F-22 Raptor — https://www.tyndall.af.mil/About/Fact-Sheets/Article/315028/f-22-raptor/
  - supercruise: supersonic, greater than Mach 1.5, without afterburner; two Pratt & Whitney F119-PW-100 turbofans with afterburners and two-dimensional thrust vectoring nozzles; 35,000-pound thrust class each
  - wingspan 44 ft 6 in; speed Mach 2 class; ceiling above 50,000 ft; one M61A2 20mm cannon with 480 rounds; air-to-air: six AIM-120 + two AIM-9 in internal bays; crew one; IOC 2005
- [JBLE-F22] JBLE F-22 fact sheet (PDF) — https://www.jble.af.mil/Portals/46/F-22%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf
  - max takeoff weight 83,500 lb; unit cost $143 million; IOC December 2005; thrust vectoring (with design, flight controls, thrust-to-weight) lets it outmaneuver current and projected aircraft; "supercruise"
- [ACC-F22] ACC fact sheet, F-22 Raptor — https://www.acc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/660143/f-22-raptor/
  - stealth (low observability); air-to-air configuration six AIM-120 AMRAAMs and two AIM-9 Sidewinders
- [NAVY-SSN] U.S. Navy fact file, Attack Submarines (SSN) — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169558/attack-submarines-ssn/
  - Seawolf class: described as very quiet, fast, well armed, with advanced sensors; eight torpedo tubes, up to 50 weapons in the torpedo room; SSN 21/22 length 353 ft; submerged displacement 9,138 tons; one nuclear reactor; speed 25+ knots; crew 140
  - Los Angeles class includes SSN 768 USS Hartford, SSN 769 USS Toledo
- [CSP-ICEX18] Commander, Submarine Force Pacific, "Submarines USS Hartford, USS Connecticut surface together in the Arctic Circle" (Mar 12, 2018) — https://www.csp.navy.mil/Media/News-Admin/Article/1465556/
  - USS Hartford (Los Angeles class) and USS Connecticut (Seawolf class) surfaced in the Beaufort Sea, Arctic Circle north of Alaska, during ICEX 2018; HMS Trenchant also took part (not used)
  - ice camp: temporary camp on a moving ice floe about 150 miles off Alaska's northern slope
  - USS Skate (SSN 578) made the first Arctic surfacing in March 1959
- [AFGSC-B2] Air Force Global Strike Command fact sheet, B-2 Spirit — https://www.afgsc.af.mil/About/Fact-Sheets/Article/630717/b-2-spirit/
  - multi-role heavy bomber; crew two pilots (B-52: five); unrefueled range about 6,000 nautical miles; low observability from reduced infrared, acoustic, electromagnetic, visual and radar signatures
  - four GE F118-GE-100 engines, 17,300 lb thrust each; wingspan 172 ft; length 69 ft; payload 60,000 lb; max takeoff weight 336,500 lb; speed high subsonic; ceiling 50,000 ft
  - first aircraft, Spirit of Missouri, delivered Dec 17, 1993; unit cost approximately $1.157 billion (fiscal 1998 constant dollars)
