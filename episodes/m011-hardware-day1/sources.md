## Official fact sheets / articles
- [NAVY-CIWS] U.S. Navy fact file, MK 15 Phalanx Close-In Weapon System — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2167831/mk-15-phalanx-close-in-weapon-system-ciws/
  - "Fast-reaction, detect-through-engage, radar guided, 20-millimeter gun weapon system"; inner layer point defense against anti-ship missiles, aircraft and littoral threats "that have penetrated other fleet defenses"
  - Rate of fire: "ASM & Aircraft: 4,500 rounds/min, Asymmetric Threats: 3,000 rounds/min"; magazine 1,550 rounds; M-61A1 Gatling gun; weight (Block 1B) 13,600 lb
  - "only deployed close-in weapon system capable of autonomously performing its own search, detect, evaluation, track, engage and kill assessment functions"
  - Block 1B adds an integrated, stabilized electro-optic sensor → counter small high-speed surface craft, aircraft, helicopters and UAS
  - Block 0 first installed 1980 on USS Coral Sea
  - Derived (arithmetic): 1,550 rounds ÷ 4,500 rounds/min ≈ 20.7 s → "about twenty seconds of fire"
- [AF-MM3] U.S. Air Force fact sheet, LGM-30G Minuteman III — https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104466/lgm-30g-minuteman-iii/
  - silo-launched; hardened silos; hardened cables to an underground launch control center; two-officer launch crews around the clock
  - ~15,000 mph (Mach 23) at burnout; range 6,000-plus miles; ceiling 700 miles; weight 79,432 lb; three solid-propellant stages
  - deployed June 1970, production cessation December 1978; inventory 400; three missile wings
- [AF-A10] U.S. Air Force fact sheet, A-10C Thunderbolt II (PDF + Davis-Monthan copy) — https://www.af.mil/Portals/1/documents/aboutus/FactSheets/A10_Thunderbolt_II.pdf · https://www.dm.af.mil/About-DM/Fact-Sheets/Article/2844049/a-10c-thunderbolt-ii/
  - "first Air Force aircraft specially designed for close air support of ground forces"
  - 30mm GAU-8/A seven-barrel Gatling gun, "can fire 3,900 rounds a minute"
  - pilots protected by titanium armor that also protects parts of the flight-control system; survives direct hits from armor-piercing and high-explosive projectiles up to 23mm
  - short takeoff and landing → operations near front lines; up to 16,000 lb mixed ordnance; 420 mph; max takeoff weight 51,000 lb
  - date deployed March 1976; inventory approximately 281 (total force)
  - "fifty years in service": March 1976 → Nov 2026 = 50 years
- [ARMY-HIMARS] Army.mil, "Army Fields HIMARS to Fourth Battalion" — https://www.army.mil/article/6260/army_fields_himars_to_fourth_battalion
  - one pod of six rockets instead of the MLRS' two pods / twelve rockets; 5,000-pound rocket pod; five-ton truck chassis; wheeled, lighter than the MLRS tracked vehicle
  - fires the same family of munitions as MLRS; built to be carried on the C-130; moved by rail or air; rolled off an aircraft, fired and reloaded onto the aircraft in a matter of minutes
- [LM-HIMARS] Lockheed Martin (manufacturer), HIMARS — https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/products/high-mobility-artillery-rocket-system.html
  - six-pack of GMLRS rockets, or two Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM), or one ATACMS missile; FMTV 5-ton truck chassis
- [NAVY-SINKEX] Navy.mil, "Multinational Partners Conducted a Multi-Domain Sinking Exercise" — https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/Article/3106575/multinational-partners-conducted-a-multi-domain-sinking-exercise/
  - decommissioned Austin-class amphibious transport dock ex-USS Denver (LPD 9) sunk in a SINKEX during RIMPAC 2022
  - waters more than 15,000 feet deep, more than 50 nautical miles north of Kauai
  - land: JGSDF and U.S. Army (Type 12 surface-to-ship missiles, practice rockets incl. HIMARS); air: Navy F/A-18F Super Hornets (VFA-41, long-range anti-ship missile), Army AH-64 Apaches (Hellfire, rockets, 30mm), Marine F/A-18C/D Hornets (VMFA-232); sea: USS Chaffee (DDG 90) Mark 45 5-inch gun
  - ship stripped of PCBs, trash, floatable materials, mercury/fluorocarbon materials; petroleum cleaned from tanks and piping
  - Denver commissioned Oct. 26, 1968; significant role in Operation Frequent Wind, April 1975 evacuation of Saigon; decommissioned Aug. 14, 2014, after 46 years of service, as the oldest deployable warship in the Navy
