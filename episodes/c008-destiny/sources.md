# THE CALL 008 — Atlantic Destiny · sources (claim → page actually opened)

## Primary
- **TSB Canada, Marine transportation safety investigation report M21A0041** — https://bst-tsb.gc.ca/eng/rapports-reports/marine/2021/m21a0041/m21a0041.html
  - Atlantic Destiny: stern trawler (fishing, factory), processes and freezes scallops; built 2002 Skagen, Denmark; operator Ocean Choice International; 31 crew; departed Riverport, NS, 17 Feb 2021 for a 21-day scallop trip; fishing near Georges Bank; title "120 nautical miles south of Yarmouth, Nova Scotia".
  - Weather: NW 40–45 kn gusting 55 kn; waves 5–8 m; air 0 °C; water ~5 °C.
  - 2 March: fishing suspended, speed reduced. ~1930 one shaft generator offline; ~1940 second offline → blackout; auxiliary generator starts; engine overspeeds; second engineer presses engine-room emergency stop, engine keeps running; <1 min later shaft generators and associated machinery fail catastrophically → fire, dense smoke, engine stops; breach in seawater piping.
  - Master briefly pressed the VHF-DSC distress button; nearby vessels reported the call was not received. 2008 Atlantic Preserver relays the distress to CCG MCTS Halifax.
  - ~2015 engine-room CO2 released, fuel shut off. ~2100 attempt to close sea bay valves; water already at deck plates; failed. Bilge pumps unavailable; emergency fire pump would not start; auxiliary generator could not be brought online.
  - ~2204 RCAF SAR airplane on scene; ~2210 pump kit dropped; first kit drifts away; second recovered.
  - 2317 RCAF SAR helicopter arrives with 2 SAR technicians; master orders evacuation of non-essential crew; helicopter leaves early (hydraulic problem) with 6 crew.
  - 3 March 0031 USCG helicopter delivers pump kit, evacuates 8; 0235 another USCG helicopter delivers pump kit, evacuates 13 incl. chief mate; 27 total taken to Yarmouth.
  - SAR pumps lost suction, strainers clogged with debris, crew entered the cold water to clear them; hoses too short; pump kits may be ineffective for vessels this size.
  - ~0530 pumping abandoned; two life rafts deployed, Jacob's ladders; ~0537 considerable list to port; ~0600 RCAF helicopter attempt — 2 hoist cables entangled in rigging, leaves; ~0700 CCGS Cape Roger arrives; ~0719 4 crew + 2 SAR techs transferred by fast rescue craft; all 31 rescued; 1036 sinks at 41°51′N 066°12′W.
  - Causes: engine management component(s) kept increasing fuel → overspeed; automatic and manual safety system failed to stop it; explosion caused fire and seawater-piping breach; flooding uncontrollable.
  - Note: times are given in the report without a zone in the parts read; the sinking time 1036 matches the USCG's 9:36 a.m. (Eastern) one hour apart, i.e., the report uses Atlantic time. Narration gives the report's clock times without a zone, and graphics say "TIMES AS GIVEN IN THE TSB REPORT".
  - TSB recorded two minor injuries; USCG/DVIDS said no injuries were reported → the script makes no injury claim.
- **TSB news release, 24 Jan 2024** — https://tsb-bst.gc.ca/eng/medias-media/communiques/marine/2024/m21a0041-20240124.html
  - Catastrophic engine failure ~120 nm south of Yarmouth; crew re-entered the CO2-sealed space several times, letting oxygen back in and allowing the fire to reignite; did not know they had to let it cool; safety concern about crew knowledge of pre- and post-release stages of CO2 systems.
- **U.S. Coast Guard release, March 3, 2021 (govdelivery)** — https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/2c4da1d
  - JRCC Halifax alerted First District at 7:05 p.m.; 143-foot Atlantic Destiny disabled, fire aboard, taking on water; Air Station Cape Cod HC-144 Ocean Sentry and two MH-60 Jayhawks; CH-149 from 14 Wing Greenwood hoisted 6 and dropped 2 SAR techs; Jayhawks hoisted 21; taken to Yarmouth; last 4 + 2 techs to CCGS Cape Roger.
- **DVIDS 785731** (video by PO3 Briana Carter, USCG courtesy) — Air Station Cape Cod MH-60 crews assisted RCAF with the rescue of the crew of Atlantic Destiny, 143-foot Canadian commercial fishing vessel, 115 nm south of Nova Scotia; all 31 rescued. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/785731
- **DVIDS 816332** (PO2/PO1 Travis Magee) — Oct. 1, 2021, Adm. Karl Schultz presents awards to members of Air Station Cape Cod involved in the rescue of 31 fishermen. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/816332

## Secondary
- **Vertical Magazine, "Atlantic Destiny"** — https://verticalmag.com/features/atlantic-destiny/
  - Jayhawks recalled from training; each Jayhawk had about two hours of fuel → flew survivors to Yarmouth rather than a headwind leg to Cape Cod; ship's 80-foot mast swung widely, hard to hold a hover; RCAF techs tended trail lines and helped people into the basket; CG6032 hoisted 8, CG6039 hoisted 13.
  - Lt. Craig Campbell: "Our helicopter is kind of limited in space. It's not really set up for that many people." / "Now we had two experienced guys tending trail lines, getting people in the basket; it really sped up our hoisting."

## Added after fact-check pass 1 (verified in pass 2)
- Vertical: CC-130H Hercules (Rescue 343) reached the ship in ~40 min over ~200 miles from Greenwood and became on-scene coordinator; CH-149 Cormorant (Rescue 907) ~80 min; Cormorant planned ~1 h in the hover, two people every five minutes; primary hoist not working → used secondary (outboard) hoist; six hoisted in three lifts; RCAF SAR techs Sgt. Brad Nisbet and Sgt. Fernando Bianco; CG6032: Lt. Travis Christy (AC), Lt. Craig Campbell (co-pilot), FM Phil Morales, swimmer Adam Via; CG6039: Lt. Cdr. Brian Kudrle, Cdr. David McCown, FM Adam Niski, swimmer Clayton Maidlow; crews planned from the start to land in Yarmouth (headwind back to Cape Cod, fuel used).
- National Fisherman (citing CBC): Phillip Morales (avionics electrician first class): "This was one of the craziest rescues that I've been a part of" — narrated only (sentence continues in the original). CG6032 hoisted 8 = the 0031 helicopter (TSB) → "first Jayhawk". Cape Roger: 205-foot fisheries patrol vessel.
- TSB: vessel carried four inflatable life rafts, two deployed ~0530; auxiliary generator restarted/running but could not be brought online, likely due to damage to the electrical distribution system; crew entered the cold water to clear pump strainers.
