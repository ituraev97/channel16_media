## 2. DVIDS 1001011: Medevac from dive yacht Turks and Caicos Explorer II, about 200 nm NW of Puerto Rico (March 25, 2026)

Primary source: USCG press release dated March 26, 2026, San Juan. https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4445250/coast-guard-medevacs-woman-passenger-from-dive-yacht-turks-and-caicos-ii-200-na/

- **Headline (exact):** "Coast Guard medevacs woman passenger from dive yacht Turks and Caicos II 200 nautical miles from Puerto Rico" (release URL above)
- **When:** "Wednesday afternoon". No clock time is given. (release) March 25, 2026 was a Wednesday, which matches the DVIDS date.
- **Who called and how:** the dive yacht asked Coast Guard Sector San Juan watchstanders for a medevac. The communication method (radio, satphone, etc.) is not stated. (release)
- **What went wrong (exact):** the yacht was "reporting one of their passengers had aspirated water while snorkeling and that her condition was deteriorating" (release). The patient was a 66-year-old woman. (release; DVIDS caption)
- **Decision:** watchstanders evaluated the case, decided she needed a higher level of care, and launched an MH-60T Jayhawk from Air Station Borinquen. (release)
- **Location:** about 200 nautical miles northwest of Puerto Rico. (release; DVIDS caption)
- **Weather/sea:** not mentioned. (release)
- **Crew actions (exact):** "the air crew deployed their rescue swimmer who assessed the patient's condition and used a rescue basket". She was hoisted aboard in the basket. (release)
- **Where taken:** Centro Medico Hospital, San Juan, Puerto Rico. Described as "safely transported". (release)
- **Quote:** Petty Officer 3rd Class Judson Swift, Aviation Survival Technician, Air Station Borinquen (release):
  - "Excellent teamwork and technical expertise aboard the aircraft enabled us to execute a seamless hoist of the patient,"
  - "We are grateful for the coordination between Sector San Juan, communications command, the vessel master"
  - "and the Centro Medico Hospital that allowed us to safely transport the patient to a higher level of care."
- **Safety advice:** none in the release.
- **Secondary source:** MilitarySpot repost (dated Mar 27, 2026) matches the release. https://www.militaryspot.com/news/coast-guard-medevacs-woman-from-dive-yacht
- **Self-harm indicators:** none. This was a snorkeling medical emergency (aspirated water).
- **Discrepancies and notes:**
  - The headline says "Turks and Caicos II" and "200 nautical miles from Puerto Rico". The body and DVIDS say "Turks and Caicos Explorer II" and "northwest of Puerto Rico". **Use "Turks and Caicos Explorer II".**
  - The DVIDS caption says only "experiencing a medical emergency". The release adds the snorkeling and aspiration cause.
  - The release ends with a stray line about "the grounding and response efforts", which looks like leftover template text. Ignore it. (https://www.militaryspot.com/news/coast-guard-medevacs-woman-from-dive-yacht)
  - Number of hoists: one basket hoist of the patient is described. The swimmer's deployment and recovery are not detailed. **[UNCERTAIN beyond that]**

## Context facts

### Aircraft
- **MH-65E Dolphin:** USCG "Short Range Recovery" helicopter that can operate from cutter flight decks. Cruise speed 148 knots, range 350 nautical miles, about 3 hours endurance. Has a digital glass cockpit with avionics common to the MH-60T, and its service life was extended to 30,000 flight hours. From the USCG acquisition fact sheet, Dec. 2024. https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/Factsheets/MH65_120224.pdf
- **MH-60T Jayhawk:** USCG "medium range recovery helicopter". Speed 170 knots, range 700 nautical miles, endurance 6.5 hours. Has surface-search radar, an electro-optical/infrared sensor, and sensor and hoist cameras. From the fact sheet, Jan. 2021. https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/Factsheets/MH-60T.pdf

### Air stations (USCG Historian's Office pages, some outdated)
- **Air Station Savannah:** established summer 1963 at Hunter Army Airfield, Savannah, GA. Coverage: "the Atlantic coast from the northern border of South Carolina to Melbourne Florida". The page's aircraft details (HH-65B) are outdated. Current cases cite the MH-65. https://www.history.uscg.mil/browse-by-topic/Aviation/Article/3062116/air-station-savannah-georgia/
- **Air Station Borinquen:** moved from San Juan in fall 1971 to Rafael Hernández Airport, on the northwest tip of Puerto Rico near Aguadilla. The page gives the Sector San Juan area as running from "the western end of Haiti to the mid-Atlantic", extending southwest of Bermuda to the north coast of South America. The page lists HH-65s, which is **outdated**: the 2026 case confirms the station now flies the MH-60T Jayhawk. https://www.history.uscg.mil/browse-by-topic/Aviation/Article/3061970/air-station-borinquen-puerto-rico/
- **Air Station North Bend:** established 1974 at North Bend Municipal Airport, Oregon. The page describes the co-located Group North Bend area as stations "from Depoe Bay to the California border". That is not exactly the air station's flight area. Waldport is just south of Depoe Bay, which is consistent. The page lists HH-65s as of 2006. https://www.history.uscg.mil/browse-by-topic/Aviation/Article/3062091/air-station-north-bend-oregon/
