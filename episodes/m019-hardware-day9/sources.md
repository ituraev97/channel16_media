## t1 — M3E1 Carl Gustaf / MAAWS (84 mm recoilless rifle)
- MAAWS = "reloadable, recoilless rocket system"; M3E1 Carl Gustaf recoilless rifle + fire-control system + backup reflex sight; Marine Corps Systems Command: rounds "out to 1,300 meters"; a gunner fires, a second Marine carries and helps load the ammunition; can fire illumination, smoke and airburst rounds; Army figure 14.2 lb for the M3E1 + 4.8 lb fire control; newest Marine recoilless rifle since the SMAW (mid-1980s) — Task & Purpose: https://taskandpurpose.com/news/marine-corps-maaws-new-recoilless-rifle/
- Swedish; design dates to the late 1940s (Carl Gustafs Stads Gevärsfaktori); 84 mm recoilless rifle — some propellant gas exits the rear, offsetting most of the recoil; the rear blast is dangerous, so it can't be fired indoors without special ammunition; M3 ~22 lb, M3E1 just over 15 lb; 75th Ranger Regiment adopted the M3 in 1988; AT4 is single-shot, M3E1 reloadable — The War Zone: https://www.twz.com/15629/the-army-rushes-a-1000-recoilless-rifles-to-troops-but-whats-a-carl-gustaf-anyway
- "84mm lightweight, man-portable, direct-fire recoilless rifle"; weight < 15 lb vs 22 lb (legacy M3); munitions incl. HEDP, HEAT, airburst HE, multi-target, thermobaric, illumination, smoke — PEO Soldier portfolio: https://peo360portfolio.com/product-details/product/210
- "84 mm reloadable, recoilless rifle"; 32nd Infantry Brigade soldiers, Fort McCoy, Wis., Oct 26, 2023 — DVIDS 912680
- Footage: 1015203 (2nd Bn, 2nd Marines, SLTE 4-26, Twentynine Palms, July 17, 2026); 985431 (BLT 1/7, 31st MEU, Camp Fuji, Japan, Oct 6, 2025 — 100–110 s not used: different launcher); 975316 (3rd Bn, 4th Marines, Arctic Edge 25, JBER, Alaska, Aug 20, 2025); 961380 (Lima Co., 3/25 Marines lead a combined MAAWS range, TRADEWINDS 25, Trinidad and Tobago, May 3, 2025 — interview section 0–320 s not used); 912680 (Fort McCoy 2023).

## t2 — KC-135 Stratotanker
- "provides the core aerial refueling capability for the United States Air Force"; also supports Navy, Marine Corps and allied aircraft; boom = primary fuel transfer method, operated by a crewmember in the rear; shuttlecock-shaped drogue attached to the boom for probe-equipped aircraft; max transfer fuel load 200,000 lb; up to 83,000 lb cargo; first production Stratotanker delivered to Castle AFB, Calif., June 1957; speed 530 mph at 30,000 ft; ceiling 50,000 ft; crew three (pilot, co-pilot, boom operator); four engines, 21,634 lb thrust each; max takeoff weight 322,500 lb; inventory active 153, ANG 171, AFR 72 (= 396) — MacDill AFB fact sheet (af.mil family): https://www.macdill.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Article/4150730/kc-135-stratotanker/

## t3 — M119 105 mm howitzer (15:00)
- M119A1: Royal Ordnance (Nottingham, England) design, originally the L118/L119 British Light Gun (script: "Royal Ordnance Light Gun"); average crew of seven; max range 11,500 m (charge 7), 14,000 m (charge 8), 19,000 m with M913 rocket-assisted projectile; max rate of fire 6 rounds/min for 2 min, sustained 3 rounds/min for 30 min; air transportable by UH-60, liftable by CH-47, air droppable; first fielded to the 7th Infantry Division, Fort Ord, Dec 1989 — FAS (Army system description): https://man.fas.org/dod-101/sys/land/m119.htm

## t5 — U-2 Dragon Lady
- High-altitude, all-weather surveillance and reconnaissance; "routinely flown at altitudes over 70,000 feet"; pilot wears a full pressure suit similar to astronauts'; low-altitude handling + bicycle-type landing gear require precise control on landing, forward visibility limited; "A second U-2 pilot normally 'chases' each landing in a high-performance vehicle", giving radio inputs for altitude and runway alignment; single-seat; GE F118-101 engine; built in secrecy by Kelly Johnson and the Lockheed Skunk Works, U-2A first flew August 1955; October 1962 photographed the Soviet nuclear missile buildup in Cuba (Cuban Missile Crisis); based at Beale AFB, Calif. — ACC fact sheet: https://www.acc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/199179/u-2stu-2s/
- Speed 410 mph; range 7,000+ miles; ceiling above 70,000 ft; span 105 ft; length 63 ft; GE F118-GE-101A turbofan, 17,000 lb thrust; 27 U-2s + 4 TU-2s — Air & Space Forces Magazine: https://www.airandspaceforces.com/weapons/u-2
- Pressure-suit shots: 507037 (Airman Magazine U-2 B-roll, 2017).
- Footage: 908150 (Beale AFB, 2023: takeoffs, landings, touch-and-goes, chase cars, shot from inside a chase car); 409122 (two U-2s landing at RAF Fairford, UK, June 2015); 507037 (Airman Magazine B-roll: a "mobile" in a chase car assists with altitude and position calls).
