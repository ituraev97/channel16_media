## Official fact sheets / articles
- [AFSOC-AC130J] AFSOC fact sheet, AC-130J Ghostrider — https://www.afsoc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/2547234/ac-130j-ghostrider/
  - mission: close air support, air interdiction and armed reconnaissance; modified C-130J; Precision Strike Package with trainable 30mm and 105mm weapons
  - standoff precision-guided munitions: GBU-39 Small Diameter Bomb, GBU-69 Small Glide Munition, AGM-114 Hellfire, AGM-176 Griffin
  - four Rolls-Royce AE 2100D3 turboprops, 4,700 shaft horsepower each; max takeoff weight 164,000 lb; range 3,000 miles (crew duty day), extendable with air refueling
  - wingspan 132 ft 7 in; length 97 ft 9 in; crew: 2 pilots, 2 combat systems officers, 4 special mission aviators (= 8)
  - date deployed July 2019; unit cost $165 million; inventory 37 active duty aircraft by FY24
- [AF-AC130J] U.S. Air Force fact sheet, AC-130J Ghostrider — https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/467756/ac-130j-ghostrider
  - fifth-generation gunship; replaced the AC-130U and AC-130W fleet; same general characteristics as above
- [RAF-F35B] Royal Air Force (UK MoD), Lightning (F-35B) — https://www.raf.mod.uk/aircraft/lightning/
  - one Pratt & Whitney F135; max speed Mach 1.6; max altitude 50,000 ft; max takeoff weight approx. 27,216 kg (60,000 lb); STOVL lets it operate from carriers, allied vessels and short airstrips
- [LM-F35B-VL] Lockheed Martin release, "F-35 Joint Strike Fighter Succeeds in First Vertical Landing" (Mar 18, 2010) — https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2010-03-18-Lockheed-Martin-F-35-Joint-Strike-Fighter-Succeeds-in-First-Vertical-Landing
  - F-35B: single Pratt & Whitney F135 engine that drives a counter-rotating Rolls-Royce LiftFan; a three-bearing swivel duct vectors engine thrust downward; under-wing roll ducts provide lateral stability (spoken: "keep it steady")
- [USMC-F35B-IOC] marines.mil, "U.S. Marine Corps declares the F-35B operational" — https://www.marines.mil/News/News-Display/Article/611657/us-marines-corps-declares-the-f-35b-operational/
  - IOC July 31, 2015; VMFA-121, Yuma, Ariz.; "first squadron in military history to become operational with any variant of the F-35"; F-35B will eventually replace AV-8B Harrier, F/A-18 Hornet and EA-6B Prowler (spoken: "was set to replace")
  - Short takeoff and vertical landing variant flown by the Marine Corps (also [RAF-F35B] STOVL)
- [NAVY-D5] U.S. Navy fact file, Trident II (D5) missile — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2169285/trident-ii-d5-missile/
  - Ohio-class submarines carry up to 20; UK Vanguard-class also carries it; three-stage, solid-fuel, inertially guided SLBM
  - range 4,000 nautical miles (4,600 statute miles); length 44 ft; weight 130,000 lb; deployed 1990; unit cost $30.9 million
  - "most survivable leg of the nuclear Triad"; life-extended D5 (D5LE) entered Fleet service in early 2017; will initially deploy on the Columbia-class (and UK Dreadnought-class)
- [AUS-M777] Australian Army, M777 155mm lightweight towed howitzer — https://army.gov.au/equipment/artillery/m777-155mm-lightweight-towed-howitzer
  - 155mm towed howitzer; weight 4,100 kg (derived: ≈ 9,040 lb → "about nine thousand pounds"); effective range 24 km conventional, 30 km improved rounds; rate of fire 2 rds/min sustained, 5 rapid
- [ARMY-M777] Army News Service, "Army fields its first light weight howitzer" (Oct 31, 2006) — https://www.army.mil/article/476/army_fields_its_first_light_weight_howitzer
  - M777 is 7,000 lb lighter than the M198 it replaces; two M777s can be carried on a C-130 at the same time; developed jointly by the Army and Marine Corps; built by BAE Systems
- [USMC-M777-2005] HQMC, "New howitzer makes thunderous debut" (May 24, 2005) — https://www.hqmc.marines.mil/News/Article/Article/551796/new-howitzer-makes-thunderous-debut
  - M-777 debuted at the Combat Center desert (Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center, Twentynine Palms, Calif.) the week before May 24, 2005; crews fired more than 1,150 rounds during the exercise week
- [NAVY-LCAC] U.S. Navy fact file, Landing Craft, Air Cushion — https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2170004/
  - payload 60 tons, 75-ton overload; can carry an M-1 tank; speed 40+ knots at full load; four TF-40 gas turbines (2 propulsion, 2 lift), 16,000 hp
  - air cushion can reach more than 70% of the world's coastline vs about 15% for conventional landing craft; crew of five
  - length 91 ft 9.5 in ("almost ninety-two feet"); beam 48 ft 4 in; operates from well deck ships (LHA, LHD, LSD, LPD, ESD)
  - first LCAC delivered 1984; total of 91 built
