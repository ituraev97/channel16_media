## t1 — FIM-92 Stinger
Page: I MEF (marines.mil), "Feel the sting with 3rd LAAD Bn" — https://www.imef.marines.mil/Media-Room/Stories/Article/Article/614725/feel-the-sting-with-3rd-laad-bn
- The FIM-92 Stinger is "a portable, infrared-homing surface-to-air missile"; can be launched from ground vehicles or helicopters.
- "maximum effective range" 26,400 feet; engages targets "at altitudes from 600 to 12,500 feet".
- Aug 8-12, 2015 Fort Irwin exercise: Marines fired 60 Stingers at remote-controlled airplanes.
Page: army-technology.com, Stinger — https://www.army-technology.com/?p=15284
- Weight about 10 kg, "15.87 kg" including the grip stock; length 1.52 m (≈ 5 ft); maximum range about five miles.
- Development began 1967 as Redeye successor; named Stinger March 1972; "initial operational capability in February 1981". Raytheon is the primary manufacturer.
Page: RTX/Raytheon, Stinger missile — https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/stinger-missile
- "lightweight, self-contained air defense system that can be rapidly deployed by ground troops".
- Adopted by "all four U.S. military services".
- 2019: U.S. Army began retrofitting Stingers with proximity fuzes, which let the missile destroy drones with direct hits or by detonating nearby.
Page: army.mil, Letterkenny Army Depot completes 72nd Avenger — https://www.army.mil/article/209435
- Avenger (AN/TWQ-1) on an M1097A1 HMMWV chassis; fire unit "carries eight Stinger missiles in two missile pods".
DVIDS 451610: Bravo Battery, 2nd LAAD Bn Marines fire FIM-92C Stingers, Vaagan Range, Orland, Norway, Feb 24, 2016, Cold Response 16 (target drones launched from a rail launcher are visible in the clip).
DVIDS 936202: 2nd LAAD Marines fire a FIM-92A Stinger at a training unmanned aerial vehicle in Sweden, Sept 6, 2024.
DVIDS 944147: 5th Bn, 4th ADA Regt Soldiers, Stinger live fire with the Avenger Air Defense Missile System and MANPADS, Nov 13, 2024, Putlos Training Area, Germany.
DVIDS 975005: 11th Airborne Division infantrymen fire the FIM-92 Stinger "for the first time ever in Indonesia" during Super Garuda Shield 25, Baturaja, Aug 26, 2025.

## t2 — F-15E Strike Eagle
Page: F-15E Strike Eagle fact sheet (Seymour Johnson AFB, af.mil) — https://www.seymourjohnson.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/F-15E-Strike-Eagle/
- "a dual-role fighter designed to perform air-to-air and air-to-ground missions"; avionics let it fight "at low altitude, day or night, and in all weather".
- Crew: two, pilot and weapon systems officer. Two Pratt & Whitney F100-PW-220 or -229 engines, 25,000 to 29,000 pounds thrust each.
- Length 63.8 ft; wingspan 42.8 ft; maximum takeoff weight 81,000 pounds; speed 1,875 mph (Mach 2.5+); ferry range 2,400 miles with conformal fuel tanks and three external tanks.
- Armament includes "eight AIM-120 AMRAAMs" (air-to-air option).
- First production F-15E delivered to the 405th TTW, Luke AFB, in April 1988.
Page: ACC F-15E fact sheet — https://www.acc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/199184/f-15e-strike-eagle/ (same crew/engine/delivery data).

## t3 — Electromagnetic Railgun
Page: pacom.mil (ONR), "Ready, Railgun!" (July 25, 2017) — https://www.pacom.mil/Media/NEWS/Article/1257851/ready-railgun-electromagnetic-weapon-primed-for-operational-demos/
- "a massive electrical pulse, rather than gunpowder or other chemical propellants"; projectiles "exceed Mach 6"; distances "over 100 nautical miles".
- Goals: 20 MJ by end of summer 2017 and 32 MJ the next year; "One MJ is roughly a one-ton vehicle moving at 160 mph."
- HVP: "next-generation, low-drag, guided" projectile usable in different gun systems. Early rep-rate multi-shot salvos at Dahlgren's Terminal Range.
Page: Military.com, "The Navy Finally Pulls the Plug on the Railgun" (July 2, 2021) — https://www.military.com/daily-news/2021/07/02/navy-finally-pulls-plug-railgun.html
- Navy said it would pause railgun R&D at the end of 2021, citing "fiscal constraints, combat system integration challenges"; program began in 2005, "a $500 million experiment".
- In 2018 then-CNO Adm. John Richardson told Congress it had not reached its promised range; the barrel was "probably the limiting case".
Page: The War Zone (twz.com), hypervelocity rounds killed off in budget — https://www.twz.com/40938/long-in-development-hypervelocity-rounds-for-navy-railguns-and-deck-guns-killed-off-in-budget
- Railgun work began in 2005; the Navy says it will keep railgun hardware and technical documentation for potential future use.
DVIDS 403786: railgun fires the Hyper Velocity Projectile during testing at NSWC Dahlgren Division (QuickLook report dated 4 Sept 2014).
DVIDS 516509: ONR and NSWC Dahlgren conduct the first shot of the railgun at the terminal range, Nov 17, 2016.
DVIDS 539084: ONR demonstrates initial rep-rate fires of multi-shot salvos at NSWC Dahlgren; "speeds that exceed Mach 6", "distances over 100 nautical miles" (2017).
DVIDS 386816: ONR "Navy Railgun Goes East" (Jan 2015) — railgun lab/firing test footage; labelled generically as test footage.

## t4 — M2 .50-caliber machine gun
Page: USMC The Basic School, Heavy Machine Gun (B3M4238) — https://www.trngcmd.marines.mil/Portals/207/Docs/TBS/B3M4238%20Heavy%20Machine%20Gun.pdf
- Developed by John M. Browning at the end of World War I; an improved version was adopted in 1933; nearly two million M2 variants produced during WWII; "mainstay" of Marine Corps heavy machine gun platoons.
- Cyclic rate 450-550 rounds per minute; maximum effective range 1,830 m; maximum range 7,400 m; total weight 128 lb (receiver 60, barrel 24, M3 tripod 44).
Page: army.mil, "M2A1 Machine Gun features greater safety, heightened lethality" — https://www.army.mil/article/92130/m2a1_machine_gun_features_greater_safety_heightened_lethality
- Nickname "Ma Deuce"; "mounts on most aircraft and vehicles" and can fire from a tripod.
- M2A1: quick-change barrel, fixed headspace and timing; new flash hider reduces muzzle flash by 95 percent.
- Army M2 inventory "exceeds 54,000 guns" (as reported in the article).
DVIDS 914247: 4th Sqn, 2nd Cavalry Regiment M2 .50-cal live fire, Grafenwoehr Training Area, Germany, Feb 29, 2024.
DVIDS 971988: 2nd Distribution Support Bn Marines fire the M2 mounted on a JLTV, Camp Lejeune, N.C., July 27, 2025.
DVIDS 997976: 12th Littoral Combat Team Marines fire Mk-19s and M2 .50-cal machine guns during a CALFEX, Rodriguez Live-Fire Complex, Republic of Korea, Feb 28, 2026 (only M2 shots used).

## t5 — NMESIS / Naval Strike Missile
Page: Naval News, "US Marines Conduct First NMESIS Launch in Two Years" (July 2023) — https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2023/07/us-marines-conduct-first-nmesis-launch-in-two-years/
- NMESIS = Navy/Marine Expeditionary Ship Interdiction System; unmanned launcher on a JLTV chassis (Oshkosh ROGUE Fires).
- NSM range "100 nmi (185 km)"; passive dual-band infrared seeker; anti-ship/land-attack cruise missile.
- Each battery: 18 launchers; 14 batteries planned; full operational capability when all 14 are fielded in 2030.
- First live fire November 2020; second August 2021 (LSE 21); June 2023 at NAS Point Mugu.
- Raytheon produces the NSM in the U.S.; Kongsberg (Norway) is the original equipment manufacturer.
Page: USNI News, "First Image of Marines' New Anti-Ship Missile Unmanned Truck Emerges" (Apr 28, 2021) — https://news.usni.org/2021/04/28/first-image-of-marines-new-anti-ship-missile-unmanned-truck-emerges
- NMESIS is a shore-based anti-ship missile vehicle; "Remotely Operated Ground Unit for Expeditionary (ROGUE) Fires" built by Oshkosh; NSM by Raytheon.
DVIDS 890582 / 890579: Fox Battery, 2nd Bn, 11th Marines, 1st Marine Division employ NMESIS at NAS Point Mugu, June 27-29, 2023; "The unmanned NMESIS ... allowing a small crew of Marines to operate in forward environments with minimal detectability"; "the first Marines to operate and fire the NMESIS".
