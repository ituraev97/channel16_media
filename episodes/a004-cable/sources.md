## Chefornak, Alaska — family on sea ice (Apr 11–12, 2026)
- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4460663/coast-guard-rescues-4-from-vessel-trapped-in-ice-near-chefornak-alaska/
  - 3 adults + 1 child, 18-foot vessel trapped on an ice floe ~10 miles west of Chefornak; subsistence seal hunting trip
  - Saturday 4:24 p.m.: Alaska State Troopers report to Coast Guard Arctic District command center; stuck on the ice for over 24 hours
  - Overnight the party freed the vessel and tried to reach a shore party that came from the village by snowmobile/snowmachine; moving ice blocked them
  - MH-60 Jayhawk + HC-130 Hercules from Air Station Kodiak; helicopter refueled in King Salmon and Bethel; on scene about 5 a.m. Sunday
  - Crew flew over 800 miles, much of it in near-zero visibility, mountainous terrain, blowing snow and icing
  - On scene: 28-degree air temperature, 29 mph winds, 800-foot cloud ceiling, 9 miles visibility
  - All four hoisted and flown back to Chefornak; no reported injuries
  - Three forms of communication aboard, including satellite-based; greatly helped locate them
- Alaska's News Source: https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/04/15/coast-guard-rescues-child-three-adults-stranded-ice-floe-near-chefornak-most-challenging-mission/
  - Lt. Cmdr. Alexis Chavarria-Aguilar, pilot-in-command: crew agreed "this was one of the most challenging missions any of us had ever flown"; "nearly every Alaska-centric aviation weather hazard imaginable" (also ADN)
- Anchorage Daily News: https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/rural-alaska/2026/04/15/subsistence-hunting-party-rescued-after-being-trapped-on-sea-ice-for-nearly-2-days-officials-say/ — "It was a long, difficult night."

## How the hoist works
- Flight mechanic operates the hoist and relays commands to the pilot, who can't see what is directly below: https://www.military.com/daily-news/2014/02/07/coast-guard-flight-mechanic-hoists-expectations.html
- Coast Guard Auxiliary "Surface Guide to Helicopter Operations" (National Response Directorate; hosted by District 7): https://www.uscga-district-7.org/pdf/operations/helicopter_ops_best_practices.pdf
  - Downwash "can blow a person overboard and blow loose gear over the side"
  - Spinning rotor blades act like an electric generator; let the basket or cable touch the boat before you touch it; never connect the cable to the boat
  - Trail line lowered for the boat crew to guide the device and keep it from swinging
  - Helicopter approaches from astern, hovers off the port side; boat holds a steady course and speed as directed by the pilot
  - Stow loose gear; lower antennas, booms, rigging, flags; sailboats furl sails; downwash is very powerful
  - Devices: basket, litter, sling; thumbs up to the hoist operator when ready
- MH-60T typically operated by a crew of four (Air Station Cape Cod): https://www.atlanticarea.uscg.mil/Our-Organization/Northeast-District/Units/Air-Station-Cape-Cod/MH60-JAYHAWK/
- MH-60T fact sheet: medium range recovery helicopter; features include hoist cameras: https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/Factsheets/MH-60T.pdf
- AST (rescue swimmer) 22-week course: 7 weeks EMT, 10 weeks rescue swimmer, 5 weeks aviation life support: https://gocoastguard.com/careers/enlisted/ast
- Damon Point kiteboarders (background, not in script): https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4574302/coast-guard-rescues-2-kiteboarders-off-damon-point/
