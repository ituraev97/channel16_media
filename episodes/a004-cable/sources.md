## Chefornak, Alaska — family on sea ice (Apr 11–12, 2026)
- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4460663/coast-guard-rescues-4-from-vessel-trapped-in-ice-near-chefornak-alaska/
  - 3 adults + 1 child, 18-foot vessel trapped on an ice floe ~10 miles west of Chefornak; subsistence seal hunting trip
  - Saturday 4:24 p.m.: Alaska State Troopers report to Coast Guard Arctic District command center; stuck on the ice for over 24 hours
  - Overnight the party freed the vessel and tried to reach a shore party that came from the village by snowmobile/snowmachine; moving ice blocked them
  - MH-60 Jayhawk + HC-130 Hercules from Air Station Kodiak; helicopter refueled in King Salmon and Bethel; on scene about 5 a.m. Sunday
  - Crew flew over 800 miles, much of it in near-zero visibility, mountainous terrain, blowing snow and icing
  - On scene: 28-degree air temperature, 29 mph winds, 800-foot cloud ceiling, 9 miles visibility
  - All four hoisted and flown back to Chefornak; no reported injuries
  - Three forms of communication aboard, including satellite-based; greatly helped locate them
- Alaska's News Source: https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/04/15/coast-guard-rescues-child-three-adults-stranded-ice-floe-near-chefornak-most-challenging-mission/
  - Lt. Cmdr. Alexis Chavarria-Aguilar, pilot-in-command: crew agreed "this was one of the most challenging missions any of us had ever flown"; "nearly every Alaska-centric aviation weather hazard imaginable" (also ADN)
- Anchorage Daily News: https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/rural-alaska/2026/04/15/subsistence-hunting-party-rescued-after-being-trapped-on-sea-ice-for-nearly-2-days-officials-say/ — "It was a long, difficult night."
- Footage: DVIDS 1002629 (PO1 Shannon Shepard, Coast Guard Arctic District), dated Apr 12, 2026 — Kodiak MH-60 rescuing 3 adults and 1 child from an 18-foot vessel trapped on an ice floe ~10 mi W of Chefornak. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1002629

## Sea Ranger — tug sinking in the Gulf of Alaska (Aug 1, 2025)
- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4264296/coast-guard-rescues-4-people-from-sinking-vessel-in-gulf-of-alaska/
- gCaptain: https://gcaptain.com/coast-guard-aircrew-rescues-four-from-sinking-tug-in-remote-gulf-of-alaska/
  - 110-foot tug Sea Ranger taking on water ~130 miles south of Cordova; Friday Aug 1, 2025
  - 10:17 a.m. first report (watchstanders in Juneau); HC-130 from Air Station Kodiak located the vessel at 1:08 p.m.; crew said not in distress; HC-130 dropped a dewatering pump and returned
  - 3:57 p.m. second report: adrift, smoke in engine room, taking on water, dewatering pumps would not start; MH-60 Jayhawk + second HC-130 launched from Kodiak
  - 7:42 p.m. helicopter on scene, hoisted all four; taken to EMS in Cordova; no injuries / no medical concerns
  - Lt. Evan Sutton (SAR mission coordinator, Arctic District): "Given its remote location in the Gulf of Alaska, this challenging rescue resulted in the best possible outcome"; crew well equipped; importance of life rafts, survival suits, long-range distress communication devices
- Footage: DVIDS 972655 (PO1 Shannon Shepard, Coast Guard Arctic District) — Kodiak MH-60 rescuing four from the tug Sea Ranger ~130 mi S of Cordova, all hoisted. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/972655

## Walrus — sailboat sinking off North Carolina (Jan 28, 2025)
- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4046975/coast-guard-rescues-two-mariners-from-sinking-sailboat-more-than-100-miles-off/
  - Two men, 32-foot sailboat Walrus, began sinking; ~103 miles east of Wilmington, N.C. (DVIDS description says ~130 miles; script says "more than 100 miles")
  - Activated a VHF-DSC distress alert and a satellite emergency notification device; registered gear
  - C-130 Hercules arrived first, dropped a dewatering pump; MH-60 Jayhawk from Air Station Elizabeth City hoisted both men; stable, no injuries; sailboat abandoned
  - Lt. Sheena Bannon (SAR mission coordinator, District Five): "I cannot stress enough how essential it is for all mariners to register their communications gear."
- WECT: https://www.wect.com/2025/01/29/coast-guard-rescues-two-people-off-wilmington-coast
  - Sailing from Beaufort, N.C., to Puerto Rico; bad weather; water leaked in, rose above floorboards; pump couldn't keep up
  - Rescue swimmer signaled the helicopter crew when to raise each person; each lifted in a basket, pulled into the helicopter, helped out, seated with a seatbelt
  - Captain: registration let the Coast Guard get "very accurate information"

## Anacapa Island, California — kayakers in the water (Aug 14–15, 2026)
- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4578045/coast-guard-rescues-2-kayakers-near-anacapa-island/
  - Call at 11:17 p.m. Friday from two people in the water; 14-foot kayak overturned ~half a mile off Arch Rock near Anacapa Island
  - Adrift / taking on water about four hours; wetsuits and life jackets; no radio; within cellphone range
  - Sector Los Angeles-Long Beach: urgent marine information broadcast; Air Station Ventura + Station Channel Islands; TowBoatUS Ventura
  - Jayhawk on scene at midnight, hoisted both from the water; to Oxnard Airport, Ventura County EMS; stable
  - Lt. Cmdr. Jason Caprioli: "This case highlights the importance of carrying multiple reliable means of communication while on the water"; urges a signaling device and VHF-FM marine radio
- Footage: DVIDS 1019642 (Coast Guard Southwest) — Air Station Ventura Jayhawk hoists two kayakers from the water off Anacapa Island, Aug 15, 2026. https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1019642

- USCG press release: https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4526017/coast-guard-rescues-injured-hiker-from-mount-larrabee-in-mount-baker-wilderness/

## How the hoist works
- Flight mechanic operates the hoist and relays commands to the pilot, who can't see what is directly below: https://www.military.com/daily-news/2014/02/07/coast-guard-flight-mechanic-hoists-expectations.html
- Coast Guard Auxiliary "Surface Guide to Helicopter Operations" (National Response Directorate; hosted by District 7): https://www.uscga-district-7.org/pdf/operations/helicopter_ops_best_practices.pdf
  - Downwash "can blow a person overboard and blow loose gear over the side"
  - Spinning rotor blades act like an electric generator; let the basket or cable touch the boat before you touch it; never connect the cable to the boat
  - Trail line lowered for the boat crew to guide the device and keep it from swinging
  - Helicopter approaches from astern, hovers off the port side; boat holds a steady course and speed as directed by the pilot
  - Stow loose gear; lower antennas, booms, rigging, flags; sailboats furl sails; downwash is very powerful
  - Devices: basket, litter, sling; thumbs up to the hoist operator when ready
- MH-60T typically operated by a crew of four (Air Station Cape Cod): https://www.atlanticarea.uscg.mil/Our-Organization/Northeast-District/Units/Air-Station-Cape-Cod/MH60-JAYHAWK/
- MH-60T fact sheet: medium range recovery helicopter; features include hoist cameras: https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-9/Acquisition%20PDFs/Factsheets/MH-60T.pdf
- AST (rescue swimmer) 22-week course: 7 weeks EMT, 10 weeks rescue swimmer, 5 weeks aviation life support: https://gocoastguard.com/careers/enlisted/ast
- Damon Point kiteboarders (background, not in script): https://www.news.uscg.mil/Press-Releases/Article/4574302/coast-guard-rescues-2-kiteboarders-off-damon-point/
